## Why event-based, not climatology

The turbidity model is offline and requires **hourly** CROCO (`C04_I02`) and WW3 together.
Actual forcing coverage today:

| Source | Coverage |
|---|---|
| WW3 `RUN_02` | Jan–Dec 2016; Jan–Feb 2022 |
| CROCO `C04_I02_GLORYS_ERA5` | Dec 2021 – Feb 2022 |
| **Usable overlap** | **Dec 2021 – Feb 2022 (3 months)** |

`C04_I01` spans 2015–2025 but is the daily-average sibling; daily means destroy the tidal
and event structure that resuspension depends on, so it cannot drive the turbidity model.

A climatology therefore requires three chained model campaigns (CROCO re-run as `I02`,
WW3 extension, turbidity ensemble) over ~50 monthly windows. An **event-based** campaign
needs ~16 windows and is *better* evidence for the skill question: continuous runs spend
most of their compute on calm periods that demonstrate nothing, whereas a stratified event
sample makes hit rate, false alarm rate, and onset timing statistically meaningful.

**Enabling fact:** `C04_I01_GLORYS_ERA5/output` holds **monthly restart files across the
full 2015–2025 record**. Any month can be restarted as `I02` and run as a short window.
Sampling the decade does not require re-running the decade.

## Scope discipline

- **Hindcast under reanalysis (ERA5) forcing only.** Operational forecast verification is
  a future item, as in Asset 1. Title and methods make the distinction explicit.
- **"Skill assessment", not "validation."**
- **Event-based.** Climatology is the v1.1 headline upgrade and the re-engagement trigger.
- **Sediment-driven turbidity only.** HAB / chlorophyll forecasting is explicitly out of
  scope per `business-model.md`. Mentioned once as roadmap, never as capability.
- **Name the mineralogy correctly.** The suspended material along the southern Gulf coast
  is fine-grained CaCO₃ (aragonite needles, high-Mg calcite), not terrigenous quartz silt.
  The model's Stokes/Shields anchoring must be presented as applied to a **carbonate** bed.
- **No recalibration per site.** Physically-anchored parameters are applied unchanged
  wherever they are evaluated (`FINDINGS.md`, next step #3).
- **No major model changes immediately before publication**, with one exception: the
  bed-mass supply limit (see *Prerequisite model work*), which is a correctness fix, not a
  tuning change.

## Models reported

- **CROCO** `C04_I02_GLORYS_ERA5` — hourly-output sibling of the Asset 1 configuration.
  Restarted per event window from `C04_I01` monthly restarts.
- **WW3** `RUN_02` — one-way coupled with CROCO surface currents and water levels. Run per
  event window.
- **Offline 3D two-class sediment transport model** (`turbidity/offline_3d_model.py`),
  `calib_02` central parameters, **5-member ensemble**.

### Ensemble design (from `calib_02/run_3d_model_ensemble.sh`, unchanged)

Members L / ML / M / MH / H, log-spaced in a single dimensionless knob `alpha`
∈ {0.5, 1/√2, 1, √2, 2}:

- `M = M_central · alpha` — linear. Most uncertain term (NTU-to-SSC conversion, thin calibration).
- `tau_cr` held fixed — constrained by Shields / cohesive-mud literature.
- `ws` held fixed — constrained by Stokes / particle-size assumptions.
- `ubr` scaled by `(1 + s_i · sigma_ubr)`, `s_i` ∈ {−1, −½, 0, +½, +1} — additive Gaussian,
  since wave forecast errors are approximately Gaussian on Hs, not log-Hs.

Member 3 is the central calibration.

### Central parameters (from `turbidity/FINDINGS.md`)

| Parameter | Value | Physical source |
|---|---|---|
| `WS_FINE` | 1e-4 m/s | Stokes, fine silt ~10 μm |
| `WS_COARSE` | 1e-3 m/s | Stokes, coarse silt ~60–80 μm |
| `TAU_CR_FINE` | 0.1 N/m² | Shields, fine silt |
| `TAU_CR_COARSE` | 0.25 N/m² | Shields, upper fine-sand range |
| `M_FINE` | 3e-4 NTU·m/s | Calibrated |
| `M_COARSE` | 3e-3 NTU·m/s | Calibrated |
| `C_BG` | 3.0 NTU | Observed calm baseline |

**Two free parameters, not seven.** The methodology chapter must carry the degeneracy
argument verbatim: steady-state concentration scales as `M / ws`, so tuning `M` and `ws`
independently is a degenerate two-for-one calibration; fixing `ws` leaves one meaningful
knob per class. A reader who sees the model *declining* to tune reads the rest of the
document differently.

**The inherited forcing skill is the single largest credibility lever in the document and
costs nothing.** Bed stress is a deterministic function of waves and near-bed currents;
Asset 1 validates both. Asset 2 can therefore state that the *drivers* of resuspension are
independently assessed, and that what remains uncertain is the sediment response. This is
the structural reason Asset 1 must be published first.

**Provenance to confirm before publication:** the `NTU_obs.csv` record and the
`postprocess_Foresea/` analysis directory. Establish whether these are commissioned client
material and whether they are ours to publish. If commissioned, model-derived output is
likely fine and client-specific extractions are not.

## The published benchmark — and why it matters

Shanableh et al. (2021) classify whiting presence/absence from **WI = wind speed / SST**
with a cutoff of 1.1 at ~87% accuracy, and report r = 0.73 between WI and whiting extent
along the UAE and Qatar coasts. Shanableh et al. (2025) extend this to
**MI = (WS²/SST)/√depth** at ~85%.

A technical reviewer will ask the obvious question: *why is a coupled CROCO + WW3 +
sediment model needed when a ratio of two numbers gets 87%?* v1.0 must answer it
quantitatively. Three defensible differentiators, all to be demonstrated rather than
asserted:

1. **Lead time.** WI and MI are diagnostic — they require observed wind and SST. The
   modelling system runs on forecast forcing. Compute WI at analysis time *and* at 1–7 day
   lead, alongside model skill at the same leads, and show how the two degrade.
2. **Depth.** WI and MI describe the surface. Desalination intakes draw from depth. The
   model resolves vertical structure via CROCO `AKs` and already outputs `C_bottom`
   separately from `C_surface`. A satellite index structurally cannot supply
   intake-depth concentration.
3. **Magnitude and location.** The indices give basin-scale presence and extent. The model
   gives concentration at a point.

Treat this as an asset, not a threat: every credible skill assessment needs a reference
forecast to beat, and this is a published, quantified, third-party baseline in exactly this
domain and phenomenon.

## Event selection protocol — pre-registered

**The event list is fixed and committed to this repository before any production run.**
This converts the weakest available criticism of a skill assessment — *"you showed us the
cases that worked"* — into a non-argument. The cost is that events which verify badly must
still be published. Pay it: nine of twelve verifying well with three honestly diagnosed is
more persuasive than twelve clean ones, because nobody believes twelve clean ones.

### Primary source

Events are drawn from the **independently published whiting catalogue of Shanableh et al.
(2019, 2021)**. Because the catalogue predates this model and was compiled by a third
party, no selection-bias defence needs to be constructed.

**Usable window: 2015–2020** — the intersection of the published catalogue coverage
(2002–2018 and 2015–2020) with `C04_I01` restart availability (2015–2025).

### Selection order

Order by hardest constraint first.

1. **Satellite usability (hard).** For each candidate event, confirm dust- and
   cloud-screened usable MODIS coverage somewhere in the event lifecycle. Tag which phase
   is visible (onset / peak / decay). Screen on aerosol and quality flags, not the cloud
   mask alone — heavy dust passes some cloud masks and can appear as spuriously high
   reflectance, which would score a hit for entirely the wrong reason.
   **Expect the usable window to be the decay phase**, 1–3 days after the wind drops. This
   is not a consolation: plume extent and clearing timescale during decay is a strong
   discriminator and lands directly on the model's known weak spot (fine-class settling).
   Use MODIS Terra and Aqua across the whole window; add Sentinel-3 OLCI (2016+, 300 m,
   better bands for turbid coastal water) where available.
2. **Stratify the survivors** across:

| Axis | Target spread | Why it is diagnostic |
|---|---|---|
| Extent | Small / medium / large across the published 12,000–60,000 km² range | Marginal events are where `tau_cr` bites and where wrong erodibility produces false alarms. Extremes are the easy cases. |
| Duration | 2–6 d (typical) and 8–11 d (long) | Probes the too-thick decay tail; long events are the only ones that exercise the bed-mass supply limit. |
| Season | Winter core (Nov–Mar, peak Feb) + ≥1 summer null | Summer stratification suppresses the surface signal; this is the differentiator case. |
| Coastal sector | NW (Kuwait, terrigenous/riverine), central west (Bahrain/Qatar), SE (Abu Dhabi) | Wind direction sets loading; uniform sediment properties is a stated limitation and sector spread shows its consequence. |
| Spring/neap | ≥1 of each | The only isolation of the current contribution to the Soulsby combined stress. |

3. **Report empty cells.** Some strata will not survive the satellite screen. Name which,
   and why.

### Null cases — non-negotiable

**Three windows with no event**: two with strong wind but WI below the 1.1 cutoff, one
calm. Without these there is no false alarm rate, and hit rate alone is trivially gamed by
a model that cries wolf. For a skeptical reviewer this is the first thing looked for, and
including it unprompted does more than any single skill number.

### Anchor

**Jan 2022 is retained** even though it falls outside the catalogue window. It carries the
only in-situ NTU record and it does not depend on satellite visibility — which matters,
because satellite-based selection is biased (see below).

### Declared sampling bias

If dust preferentially obscures the largest events, a satellite-selected sample skews
toward weaker ones and apparent skill is flattered. **Report the wind-magnitude
distribution of the selected events against the full ERA5 distribution over 2015–2020**, so
a reader can see the skew and judge it. This is the surviving purpose of the ERA5
screening work — sample characterisation and null-case construction, not event selection.

### Target count

**12 events + 3 nulls + Jan 2022 anchor = 16 monthly windows.**

## Prerequisite model work

All of this precedes the production campaign. Nothing here is optional.

1. **Per-class bed-mass supply limit** (`m_bed[ic, j, i]`, decremented by erosion,
   replenished by deposition). `FINDINGS.md` next step #1. Erosion is currently unbounded
   per cell and the hotspot at (j=98, i=290) drifts monotonically. Harmless for a
   one-month point extraction; fatal across 16 windows with spatial analysis. **Hard
   blocker — do not launch compute before this lands.**
2. **Objective metrics on the existing Jan 2022 baseline.** `FINDINGS.md` currently records
   model–obs agreement as *"eyeballed"*. A published report cannot. Compute median calm
   NTU, mean event peak, hourly-averaged RMSE, bias, and event hit/false-alarm rate. Record
   as the baseline regression score against which every future version is measured.
3. **Slimmed `I02` output configuration.** `offline_3d_model.py` reads `AKs`, `u`, `v` and
   near-bed velocities — not `temp`, `salt`, or the other 3D tracers. A turbidity-forcing
   output config roughly halves per-month volume.
4. **Streaming pipeline.** Run CROCO month → run WW3 month → run turbidity ensemble → keep
   the 2D turbidity output → **delete the 3D CROCO**. Do not accumulate.

## Compute and storage budget

- **16 monthly windows** × (CROCO `I02` + WW3 `RUN_02` + 5-member turbidity ensemble).
- CROCO hourly output is **~12 GB/month** at present (`croco_avg_Y2022M01.nc`); ~6 GB
  slimmed.
- Turbidity output (`C_surface`, `C_bottom`, `C_bottom_per_class`, `tau_max`) is 2D and
  small.
- **With streaming, peak storage stays ~25 GB** against the 100 GB volume budget.
- Restarts come from existing `C04_I01` monthly files — no re-run of the decade.
- 2016 events are cheapest: WW3 `RUN_02` already covers that year.

**The campaign runs on wall-clock time while the writing proceeds in parallel.** At roughly
one working day per week this is the right shape — the machine does the work that cannot be
done in evenings.

## Known limitations to state explicitly

Carried from `FINDINGS.md` and extended. Reproducing these — including the *"What NOT to do
next"* discipline — is more persuasive to a metocean reviewer than any skill number.

- Single-point, single-month, single-season in-situ calibration; statistically
  under-constrained.
- Uniform sediment properties; no spatial variation in `M`, `tau_cr`, or grain size.
- Inter-event tail slightly too thick. Note that carbonate needle shape settles *slower*
  than volume-equivalent spheres, so grain shape cannot explain it — which points at
  flocculation, consistent with the original hypothesis.
- Bed stress errors not separately quantified; they propagate from WW3 and CROCO (whose
  skill is reported in Asset 1).
- **Event-based runs require an assumed initial bed state.** With the supply limit
  implemented, bed mass carries history. Spin up one month ahead of each event and state
  the assumption. Continuous bed evolution is a v1.1 item.
- Validation of `C_surface` against satellite validates forcing and mixing, **not** the
  intake-depth concentration that is the operationally relevant quantity.
- Whiting is a surface-visible proxy. Sub-surface resuspension without a surface signature
  is not observable by this evidence base.

## v1.1 roadmap (for the limitations chapter)

- **Depth-resolved exposure climatology** — the headline upgrade. Requires multi-year `I02`
  and WW3; positions as the concentration- and depth-resolved complement to Shanableh et
  al. (2019)'s published surface-extent record, and can be validated against it.
- **Continuous multi-year bed evolution**, removing the assumed initial bed state.
- **Client in-situ ML correction** at instrumented sites — the envelope collapses where
  observations exist.
- **Carbonate-specific settling and flocculation** rather than Stokes spheres.
- **Spatially varying erodibility** once a second observation site is available (do not add
  sediment classes before then — extra degrees of freedom cannot be constrained from one
  record).
- **Operational forecast verification** once the archive accumulates.

## Commercial framing note

The offer this document supports:

> *Without site data we give you a bounded envelope. Give us your intake logs and the
> envelope collapses.*

The ensemble is the product. Weak absolute calibration becomes an honest, useful, and
structurally correct reason for the client to share data — which is the bridge to the ML
correction work and the per-client moat.

**Seasonality caution for sales copy, not for this document:** surface whiting is
essentially a November–March phenomenon. A year-round subscription pitched on turbidity
alone invites the question of what is being paid for in July. The honest answers are the
subsurface stratified events of Chapter 7, and a season predictable enough to plan around.
Decide that framing deliberately.
